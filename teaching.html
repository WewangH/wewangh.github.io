Teaching

Being an educator is one of the main reasons I chose an academic career. Accounting is the language in which economic activity is recorded, communicated, and evaluated, and as artificial intelligence reshapes how that language is used in practice, I want to help rethink what we teach and how we teach it, so that students are ready for the profession and the future of work that AI is shaping.

Teaching Interests

Data Analytics (including machine learning, AI, and natural language processing), Managerial Accounting, Financial Accounting, and Financial Statement Analysis and Valuation. I am also happy to teach or develop other accounting and analytics courses.

In each of these courses I am prepared to teach the classic foundations and equally eager to explore with students how AI is changing accounting practice. In a data analytics course, I would start from a framework for how AI models work and where they fail, then move to working with AI to generate new insights from accounting data. In financial statement analysis and valuation, students can use AI tools to pull data and build models far more efficiently, which reserves class time for what AI cannot do for them: judging the quality of financial reporting and the assumptions behind a model, and forming investment decisions of their own. In managerial accounting, my research on AI and the allocation of decision rights, and on AI-based interviews as a control mechanism, offers timely examples of how AI is changing what firms measure, who decides, and how control systems adapt.

Teaching and Learning Together

I have always liked the Chinese saying jiao xue xiang zhang: teaching and learning promote each other. Teaching deepens the teacher’s own understanding, and in an era in which so much is being reshaped by AI, students and practitioners bring new tools and use cases to the classroom as quickly as we bring frameworks. I look forward to a collaborative learning journey with my students, in which we make sense of these changes together.
